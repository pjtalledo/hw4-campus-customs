# Problem 10 — Design & Styling

## What Changed

**Typography & color system** (`index.css`): added a display/body font pairing — Fraunces (a warm collegiate serif) for all headings and prices, Inter for body text — replacing the generic system-font stack. Refined the existing Yale blue/gold tokens (`--primary-color`, `--accent-color`) into a fuller system: `--accent-light`/`--accent-dark` for hover states, `--radius`/`--radius-sm` tokens, a shared `--ease` cubic-bezier for consistent motion, and `--shadow-gold` for gold-glow hover states. Also removed three stray leftover pink shadow colors (`rgba(255,20,147,...)`) in `ChatWidget.css`, `AboutUs.css`, and `Auth.css` that were inconsistent with the Yale blue/gold identity used everywhere else, replacing them with the matching blue/gold tokens.

**Navigation** (`Navigation.tsx/css`): added a circular gold "Y" crest badge next to the wordmark (rotates slightly on hover), a gold bottom border on the header, and an animated underline that sweeps in under nav links on hover instead of an abrupt color change.

**Home hero** (`Home.tsx/css`): added an eyebrow line ("Boola Boola · Est. for Yale Bulldogs") above the headline for personality, upgraded the H1 to the display serif, added a subtle two-tone radial-gradient watermark behind the hero and Products header (`.yale-crest-bg` utility — reused in both places for a consistent storefront identity rather than one-off effects), and staggered fade-up entrance animation on the hero text and featured product cards.

**Product discovery** (`Products.css`, `ProductCard.tsx/css`): the Problem 9 filter panel became a real card (white background, border, shadow) with uppercase micro-labels instead of plain inline controls. Product cards gained a rounded-corner/border treatment, a gold accent border on hover, a slightly stronger image zoom, a dashed divider above the price/CTA row, and a new **in-stock/out-of-stock badge** in the image corner (added an optional `in_stock` prop to the shared `ProductCard`, wired from the Problem 9 `in_stock` field — purely additive, so the chat-widget's product cards, which don't have this field, are unaffected). The empty-filter state got a small paw-print glyph and a fade-in instead of a flat line of text. Cards animate in with a staggered fade-up.

**Product detail** (`ProductDetail.css`): display-serif price and title, card-style image frame with shadow, gradient "Add to Cart" button with lift-on-hover, and hover motion on the search-tag pills.

**AI shopping assistant** (`ChatWidget.css`, `ChatWidget.tsx` logic untouched): this was the main focus. The toggle button now has a gold ring with a slow breathing pulse animation (so it reads as "a concierge is available," not a static icon) and rotates slightly on hover. The chat window header got a gold bottom border and a small circular "Y" badge inline with the title, visually matching the nav crest so the assistant feels like it belongs to the same brand rather than a bolted-on widget. User/assistant bubbles were redesigned with asymmetric corner radii (iMessage-style "tail" effect) — user bubbles in the blue gradient, assistant bubbles in white with a soft border/shadow — so the existing user/assistant visual distinction (required for restored Problem 8 history) is clearer, not just color-coded. The thinking indicator's three dots now use the gold accent color instead of a flat gray. The composer (textarea + Send button) got rounded-10px inputs, a gold focus ring, and the Send button is a blue gradient pill that lifts with a gold glow on hover — and visibly grays out and shows `cursor: not-allowed` while disabled, reinforcing the existing duplicate-send guard from Problem 9. The dynamic search-results panel (Problem 7/9's dismiss/replace behavior, untouched) got the same card treatment as the Products page filter panel and a top-in slide/fade entrance.

**Motion**: a shared `fadeInUp`/`fadeIn` keyframe pair and `--ease` curve are reused across the hero, product grids, chat window open/close, chat messages, and empty states, so motion feels like one coherent system rather than ad-hoc per-component animation. All animations are short (0.3–0.5s) and single-shot (no looping) except the two intentional "alive" signals — the toggle button's pulse ring and the thinking dots — which are the only continuous motion on the page, so nothing distracts from reading or shopping.

**Memorable elements** (the two the rubric asks for): (1) the gold "Y" crest badge reused identically in the nav brand, the chat header, and the hero watermark — a small recurring visual signature rather than a generic template logo; (2) the "Boola Boola" hero eyebrow line, a real piece of Yale fight-song vocabulary that ties directly to the catalogue (there is a real "Boola Boola T-Shirt" product), giving the storefront an authentic, specific voice instead of generic "Welcome to our store" copy.

## Why This Should Help Customers Stick Around and Buy

- **Faster product discovery, more confidence to click**: the in-stock badge and clearer price typography let a shopper immediately see "is this available and how much" without opening the detail page, reducing wasted clicks and dead-end visits to out-of-stock items.
- **The filter panel now looks like a tool, not an afterthought**: a bordered, shadowed card with clear labels signals "this is a real feature" and invites use, which directly supports the Problem 9 goal of shoppers self-serving by budget/category/stock instead of abandoning a long unfiltered grid.
- **The chat assistant now looks trustworthy and alive**: a breathing toggle button and branded header reduce the "is this just a generic FAQ bot" hesitation that makes shoppers ignore chat widgets. Clearer message bubbles and a visible thinking state (carried over from Problem 9, now better styled) reduce the chance a shopper closes the widget thinking it's frozen mid-answer — directly protecting the investment made in Problems 6–9's grounded tools (price/stock/recommendations/variant availability) by making sure people actually use the chat long enough to benefit from it.
- **Consistent brand recall increases return visits**: the repeated crest motif and a specific, Yale-authentic voice (vs. generic placeholder copy) make the storefront memorable rather than interchangeable with any other Bootstrap-style shop — the kind of detail that turns a one-time visitor into a repeat shopper.
